# 06 — Personality, Iconography, Illustrations & Widgets

## Distinctive does not mean unfamiliar

Use familiar navigation and interaction foundations, then create distinctiveness through execution: motion, writing, illustration, visual identity, and product-specific workflows.

## One memorable interaction

Choose one central product interaction and give it disproportionate care. This may be logging, scanning, completing, capturing, or adding.

Refine its transition, haptic, copy, processing state, and completion feedback.

## Personality at low-risk moments

Add more expression in:
- onboarding;
- empty states;
- loading;
- success;
- achievements;
- widgets;
- illustrations.

Keep critical workflows familiar and clear.

## Illustration language

If illustrations are used, define a consistent style across line weight, shape language, palette, proportions, texture, perspective, and detail level.

For generated assets, approve a canonical style first and generate variations from that reference rather than prompting each asset from scratch.

## Mascots

A mascot can become a UI primitive for searching, celebrating, thinking, empty, error, progress, or success states—but only when it genuinely fits the brand.

## Iconography

Use one coherent icon family where possible. Familiar actions should use familiar symbols.

A useful navigation pattern is outline/inactive → filled/active when the selected-state language supports it.

Review icon optical size together; identical bounding boxes do not guarantee equal perceived size.

## Widgets

Consider widgets when the product contains glanceable, frequently updated information or a recurring quick action.

A widget should answer one useful question instantly, such as:
- How much do I have left today?
- What is my next task?
- What is my current streak?

Do not shrink the entire home screen into a widget.

Where supported, deep-link the widget into the likely next context rather than generic Home.

## Platform surfaces

Evaluate home-screen widgets, lock-screen widgets, Live Activities, watch experiences, and shortcuts only when they solve a frequent need. These add engineering and maintenance cost.

External surfaces should reuse the product's typography, iconography, semantic colors, illustration language, and terminology while adapting the layout to the platform.
